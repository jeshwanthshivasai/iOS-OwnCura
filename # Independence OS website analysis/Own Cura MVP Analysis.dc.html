00 — IN PLAIN WORDS FIRST

What is this product, simply?

1. “What is my clinic worth?”

A doctor who owns a clinic types in a few facts (specialty, revenue, staff). The site shows a price range — like Zillow shows a house price. The range is honest because it comes from real clinic sales Pratham's team has actually seen.

2. “What do I do next?”

After the number, four choices side by side: keep growing alone · get help running it · sell it quietly (maybe to Pratham's own clinic company) · meet partners. Selling is one option, never a push — so it never feels like a broker's site.

3. “AI does my paperwork” — later

The AI helpers (note-taking, billing, scheduling) are not ready yet. The site says “coming soon — join the list.” The signup list proves doctors want it.

The MVP = build only parts 1 and 2 properly, put part 3 up as a signup list, and get real doctors through it. The big marketplace from the call comes later, once this small thing works.

JARGON USED BELOW, TRANSLATED
Wedge — the one small thing you build first to get in the door.
Comps / comparables — past clinic sales you compare against to estimate a price.
CIM — the sales brochure a clinic sends when it's for sale (its numbers are optimistic).
EBITDA / SDE — two ways of measuring a clinic's yearly profit.
Multiple — price ÷ profit. “Sold at 4x” = price was 4 years of profit.
The bracket / range — “worth $1.2M–$1.8M,” not one fake-precise number.
Funnel — the steps a visitor walks: land → fill form → see range → pick path → talk.
Traction / conversion — proof people use it: visitors who finish and book a call.
Broker — the middleman who sells clinics for a fee; brokers bring the first listings.
PE / private equity — investors who buy companies; Pratham wants their money to buy clinic chains.
Persona — a type of user (seller-doctor, buyer, nurse, broker…).
Phase 0 / 1 / 2 — build order: internal tool → invite-only test → open to everyone.

THE VALUATION ENGINE — THE REAL PRODUCT
  • NDA review complete — CIM data + Roots clinic financials confirmed usable. (But see the flag below.)
  • ~15–20 data points — enough for a comparables engine, not a trained model. A model fit to 15 points is false precision.
  • Sources are not equal — CIMs are asking-side, optimistic; Roots' trailing financials are realized and weighted above them.
  • Three buckets, never blended — Closed/Acquired · Evaluated/Passed · Market Listings. Every output traceable to its bucket.
  • The bracket — EBITDA/SDE multiple per comp, segmented by specialty + size band; the 25th–75th percentile spread becomes the quoted range.
  • Honesty state — one or two comps in a segment → say “insufficient comparable data for this specialty yet.”
  • Feedback loop — every close is added to the set and checked against the bracket that would have been quoted. Hit rate = credibility over time.
  • Build order — manual extraction into a structured template first; automation later.
GO-TO-MARKET & METHOD
  • Confidentiality is the product — the public “N people interested” feed is dead. Replaced by a private seller signal (“3 qualified buyers requested information this week”) and anonymized aggregate signals for buyer marketing.
  • Brokers — lead with the track record (“9–10 acquisitions over 15 years”), 20–30 emails first, separate sending subdomain, no scraping listing platforms.
  • Buyers — concrete and real (“6 practices in TX/CA exploring a next step”), physician-ownership economics on LinkedIn, no paid spend until conversion is known.
  • Weekly cycles — one hypothesis, one channel, one number, one end-of-week paragraph. Add features only when the current one works.
  • Phasing — 0: internal engine + landing + waitlist. 1: closed broker/buyer batch, manual matching. 2: open up, only on proven conversion — never on a calendar date.

04 — THE TENSION TO RESOLVE

The call says platform. The doc says wedge.

PRATHAM, IN THE CALL

Six to seven personas. Dashboards and profiles for seven agent owners. Patients, clinics, professionals, buyers, sellers, equity, employment — everything Shopify-scale, described at once.

THE DOC, V4

Phase 0 is a valuation engine, a landing page, and a waitlist. Shopify started as one snowboard shop's storefront; the ecosystem came after merchants were living inside a working core. Build Pillars 1–2 well; sequence everything else behind proof.

“The temptation to build toward the full platform vision directly will resurface… the test is the same one Shopify would have failed in 2006: is the core product working well enough, for enough real practices, to justify the next layer.”

— the doc, predicting its own failure mode

Resolution: map wide, build narrow. Bring a persona map showing where all seven personas eventually live — then build the seller wedge only. That answer is faithful to both the call and the doc, and it is the sequencing judgment the whole exercise is testing.
