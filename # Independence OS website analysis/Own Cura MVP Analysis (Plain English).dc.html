01 — THE STORY IN ONE MINUTE

A company that buys clinics wants a website that helps doctors.

Pratham's company (Independence OS, with a sister company called Roots Health) owns and runs real medical clinics in Texas and California — about 9–10 of them, with more being considered. They're also building AI software to handle the boring office work in those clinics: writing down what the doctor and patient said, sending bills to insurance, booking appointments.

Their money plan: buy clinics → run them better with the AI software → the clinics earn more → use that success to raise money from big investors → buy even more clinics. The AI software is the secret sauce; the clinics are the proof it works.

The new website — called Castle in the call, Own Cura in the document (same thing, two names — worth confirming) — is the public front door. It's where an independent doctor who owns a clinic first meets the company. The dream version is huge: a place to buy clinics, sell clinics, find jobs, get the AI tools. The question is what the first version should be.

03 — WHAT HIS DOCUMENT SAYS

“Help doctors grow” — with selling as just one option.

The document (Own Cura v4) makes one big decision: the site must never feel like a middleman whose real goal is flipping your clinic for a fee. Doctors distrust those sites. Instead, the promise is: “Own Cura helps independent practices grow — however that looks for you.” Selling your clinic is one of several doors, and the fact that Roots Health (their own company) might be the buyer is said openly, as a strength — “we run clinics ourselves, so we know what one is worth.”

PART 1 · BUILD NOW
“What is my clinic worth?”

A doctor answers ~10 questions and gets a price range — like Zillow for clinics. The range comes from real clinic sales the team has seen with their own eyes: deals they closed, deals they walked away from, and listings on the market. About 15–20 past sales exist to compare against — enough for an honest estimate, not enough for fancy AI, so the doc says: don't pretend it's AI.

PART 2 · BUILD NOW
“What do I do next?”

After the number, four choices shown as equals: keep growing on your own · bring in help to run it · quietly explore selling (Roots named as a possible buyer) · meet partners. Nothing pushes you toward selling — that's the whole point.

PART 3 · LATER
“AI does my paperwork”

The AI helpers (note-taking, billing, scheduling) aren't running anywhere yet — not even in their own clinics. So the site only says “coming soon — join the list.” The signup list itself is useful: it proves which doctors want it.

HOW THE PRICE RANGE STAYS HONEST
  • Show a range (“$1.2M–$1.8M”), never one exact number — one number would be fake precision.
  • Trust real deals more than sales brochures — a clinic's own for-sale brochure always exaggerates.
  • If there are too few similar past sales, say so — “not enough data for your specialty yet” beats a made-up number.
  • Every time a real sale closes, check the tool's guess against reality — being right over time is what builds the reputation.
RULES THE DOC SETS FOR ITSELF
  • Never show publicly who's interested in which clinic — privacy is the product. Only the seller sees “3 serious buyers asked about you this week.”
  • Never claim the AI tools are live when they aren't.
  • Don't secretly copy listings from other websites.
  • Test one idea at a time, measure one number a week, only add features when the current one works.

“We're building AI that handles scheduling, billing, and patient messages. Join the list.” Asks their specialty and clinic size — so the list doubles as proof of demand for investors.

06 — HOW TO GET THE FIRST USERS

Twenty careful emails before any advertising.

THE THREE MOVES
  • Email 20–30 clinic brokers (the middlemen who already know which clinics want to sell). Lead with the real track record: “we've bought 9–10 clinics over 15 years.” See how many reply before sending more.
  • Post on LinkedIn about the money side of owning a clinic — real numbers doctors care about, not generic tech hype. This is where your video and content skills come in.
  • Spend zero on ads until we know what fraction of visitors actually book a call. Paying for traffic into an unproven page burns money.
THE WEEKLY RHYTHM (FROM HIS DOC)

Each week: try one idea, on one channel, measure one number, write one paragraph about what happened. Only add something new when the current thing works. Week one: put the price tool live, send the first broker emails, count replies and booked calls.
