Marketplace and business model
• Oncura functions as a marketplace for independent medical practices, facilitating both the buying and selling of clinics.
• Roots Health operates as a Management Services Organization (MSO) to handle administrative and operational strategies for acquired clinics.
• Strategy includes partnering with external MSOs in Texas and California to scale marketplace liquidity rather than relying solely on internal assets.
Valuation engine development
• Developing a predictive valuation engine using public government data to estimate clinic worth without requiring initial user input.
• Platform will allow owners to refine valuation estimates by providing specific operational metrics like EBITDA and employee counts.
Strategic roadmap and GTM
• Targeting November 15 to begin deploying AI agent stacks within existing Roots Health clinics.
• Adopting a GTM strategy using curated email outreach for buyer engagement to prioritize privacy over open site browsing.
• Operations currently focus on pediatric and family practice clinics within Texas and California.
• Long-term goal is to operate as a private equity entity by acquiring clinics, optimizing operations with AI, and demonstrating profit for future investors.
Compliance and branding
• Confirmed CPOM compliance, as clinical ownership is maintained by a practicing physician.
• Official platform name is "Oncura"; "Castle" was identified as a discontinued internal conceptual metaphor.

• Clinic Valuation Engine Mechanics: Jeshwanth Shiva Sai initially proposed determining a clinic's worth as a price range, rather than a fixed number, by asking clinic owners 10 to 15 questions. Pratham Singh countered by comparing the intended mechanism to Zillow's real estate estimation model, explaining that Zillow predicts valuation ranges upfront using publicly available data without requiring initial user questions (00:12:10). Pratham Singh stated that the platform will utilize public data from government websites, such as Medicare, to pre-emptively estimate and display a valuation range for listed and unlisted clinics based on regional factors, patient panels, and patient types (00:12:59). For unlisted clinics, a notice will indicate the owner has not explicitly put the clinic up for sale. While the long-term vision for the marketplace requires expanding across all medical specialties and US states, they will initiate the platform using only their current specialized dataset (00:15:56).
• AI Agent Stack and Long-Term Platform Vision: Pratham Singh detailed that the company is building an AI model stack termed the "agent stack" via OwnCura. The strategic roadmap involves deploying and proving the viability of this thesis within their own clinics first, before selling the AI agent stack as a standalone product to external independent clinics through OwnCura. Pratham Singh emphasized that OwnCura is designed to serve as the primary foundational channel linking independent MSOs with the broader United States healthcare market (00:16:55).
• Minimum Viable Product (MVP) Personas and Customer Journey: Jeshwanth Shiva Sai defined five distinct user personas for the Minimum Viable Product (MVP), which include doctors, nurses, clinic assistants, buyers, and sellers (00:16:55). Due to privacy constraints in healthcare, buyers will not browse clinics like a traditional e-commerce shopping site. Instead, they will receive curated emails listing available practices, such as the six current practices located in Texas and California (00:18:02). Jeshwanth Shiva Sai mapped out the MVP user journey, where a customer arrives, views an automated evaluation range, updates data for a re-evaluation if necessary, and then selects a growth track. The available tracks include growing independently, receiving operational assistance from the platform, pursuing a direct acquisition by Roots Health, or meeting partner MSOs to collaborate. Private sellers and buyers will then negotiate the final steps. Pratham Singh agreed that this plan aligns with their vision, noting that the development of the marketplace is an iterative process driven by analyzing website traction data (00:18:51).

Furthermore, this system will facilitate recruitment, allowing expanding clinics or retiring physicians to find independent physicians to take over their practices (00:21:24). Pratham Singh clarified that these hiring and educational features will be deferred to later iterations, as the immediate priority remains the valuation engine and establishing the buyer-side partnerships (00:22:39).

Jeshwanth Shiva Sai raised a query regarding compliance with the Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM) doctrine, noting their research indicates that non-physicians, commercial corporations, and private equity firms are legally prohibited from practicing medicine. Pratham Singh resolved this compliance question by clarifying that the legal owner of Roots Health is Ravi&rsquo;s wife, who is a licensed medical practitioner, thereby satisfying CPOM legal requirements (00:28:52).
• Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategy and Private Equity Model: Jeshwanth Shiva Sai outlined a Go-To-Market (GTM) strategy focused on weekly feature experimentation, sending outbound emails to 20 or 30 clinic brokers each week, and posting organic content on LinkedIn detailing the financial aspects of clinic ownership. This approach aims to drive visitors to book calls without relying on paid advertising (00:24:45). Pratham Singh approved of the LinkedIn and emailing tactics but advised against focusing exclusively on traditional clinic brokers, as OwnCura is designed as a comprehensive growth platform rather than a standard brokerage (00:25:46). Pratham Singh explained that the ultimate goal is for the marketplace to function similarly to a Private Equity (PE) firm (00:29:58). The platform will facilitate the acquisition of a large volume of clinics, deploy verified AI agent stacks across them to maximize profits, and present these visible metrics to future institutional investors, comparing their intended structure to Blackstone's operation with BlackRock as an investor (00:31:05).
