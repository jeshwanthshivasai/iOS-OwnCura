01 · Executive summary

A quiet, copy-driven, one-page site with one great idea in it.

What it is. A single long-scroll page (nav anchors: Overview · Products · Vision · Team) for a pre-launch healthcare AI company. The pitch: we own and operate real clinics in Texas and California, and we build the ten-product AI operating system that runs them — proven on our own patients and billing before anyone else's.
The signature move. There is almost no imagery. The page's only "visuals" are ten small self-animating product simulations — live counters, transcripts writing themselves, claims moving through a pipeline — reused three times (hero collage, product rail, product detail). The demo data is one coherent fictional morning around one patient, Maria Reyes.
The gap. Structurally it reads as a static editorial page: no scroll choreography implied, no dimension, no conversion surface, the same ten vignettes shown three times, and the most cinematic content (the thesis) is the least designed — four paragraphs of plain text. That is the opening for the rebuild.

The content and narrative are already strong enough to carry an awwwards-level site. What is missing is not story — it is staging.

06 · Copy system & nitty-gritty catches

"One" is the load-bearing word.

Voice rules (keep these)
The "one" refrain: One operating system · One record, shared · One system, several products · One system, growing outward · One operating system, end to end · Everything runs around one practice · one shared record. It is the argument (integration) restated as rhythm.

Headline grammar: short declaratives, sentence case, closed with periods. No exclamation marks anywhere. Product taglines pivot on one italic word (shared, medicine, writes itself, picks up, slips, clean, fills itself, runs out, scale, visits).

The refrain of proof: "proven in our own clinics first" recurs five-plus times in different phrasings — it is the site's spine.

Register: plain, unhedged, faintly literary ("Record the visit. Finish the chart. Go home."). Domain vocabulary used without apology: HEDIS, HCC, CO-16, SOAP, par levels.
QA catches (fix in the rebuild)
· "personalized" and "personalised" sit two sentences apart in the hero; "personalised" again in Bishop. Pick US spelling.
· Fairmont Family Practice links to rootshealth.org/our-work/ — a third-party page, almost certainly a leftover.
· Babies 2 Teens Pediatrics is the only marquee item with no link.
· Five of ten practices show a category where the others show a city.
· Nav says "Vision"; the section eyebrows say "Approach" and "Thesis" — label mismatch.
· Four H1-level headings on one document.
· Vignette copy drifts between instances (Tracked / Items tracked; Patient / User) — fine as variants, worth making deliberate.
· All five phone numbers are +91 on a site selling to American practices; no US contact shown.
· No CTA in nav or hero; first invitation to act arrives ~16 beats down the page.

08 · Technical read

What the wire shows, and what needs a browser.

VERIFIEDSingle HTML document; all navigation is hash anchors (#overview #products #vision #team). Apex and www serve identical content.
VERIFIEDBehind Cloudflare — email addresses use /cdn-cgi/l/email-protection endpoints. Hosting is consistent with Cloudflare Pages or similar static origin.
VERIFIEDThe practice marquee duplicates its ten items twice in the DOM — the standard CSS infinite-marquee construction.
INFERREDVignettes are JS-driven loops (call timer at 00:00, "Live ingest 21/s", cycling status states), rendered as DOM/CSS cards. No evidence of canvas or WebGL anywhere.
INFERREDStatic build, no route-based framework (no per-page URLs, no hydration artifacts in extracted content). Light scheme declared; serif/editorial treatment suggested by the writing but not verifiable from text.
UNSEENBrowser console, network waterfall, fonts, exact palette, animation timing/easing, Lighthouse scores, responsive behavior. My fetcher reads content; it does not execute JavaScript. To close the gap: a 30-second screen recording of a full scroll (desktop + phone), or DevTools screenshots (Console + Network + a HAR export), or import the repo — any one of these and I will fold a fidelity addendum into this dossier. If the rebuild is a fresh design anyway, we can skip fidelity capture entirely.
09 · Critique against the awwwards bar

Strong bones, no staging.

Worth keeping
· Copy discipline — the "one" refrain, italic pivots, unhedged declaratives.
· The vignette system — products demoing themselves beats any screenshot.
· One coherent demo morning + the Maria Reyes thread — a ready-made narrative spine.
· Clinic-at-the-center mental model — already a spatial idea, begging for dimension.
· Real-world texture: actual practices, real denial codes, named people.
Where it falls short of the bar
· No scroll choreography — nineteen beats stacked, none staged; nothing earns the scroll.
· The ten vignettes appear three times each — repetition dilutes the one great idea.
· Zero depth: no WebGL, no camera, no light; a flat card world.
· The thesis — the most cinematic writing on the page — is a wall of text.
· Diagrams (orbit, layers, hub) are static; they describe motion they don't perform.
· No conversion surface, no OG cards, +91-only contact for a US audience.
10 · Rebuild opportunity map — brainstorm fodder

Ten stagings of what the page already says.

Original concepts built on their existing narrative devices — not clones of anything. Each is a scroll-driven scene; two or three of these plus quiet editorial sections is a full site. React to these and I'll take the survivors to moodboards and motion prototypes.

A · The breathing machine
Hero WebGL organism for "machines for human flourishing": a particle field that breathes at a resting heart rate, resolving between machine-grid and organic pulse as you scroll. The duality is the brand in one shader.
B · Maria's morning
The whole overview as one continuous scroll-scrubbed dolly through a stylized 3D clinic, 8:30 to close. Each room is a product; the existing vignettes anchor to walls and desks. The clock is the scrollbar.
C · Foundry gravity
Every event on the page — note written, claim paid, reorder placed — emits a particle that streams into one persistent record-plane that travels with you. Scroll = data accreting into one record. "One record, shared," performed.
D · The orbit, for real
Their radial ecosystem diagram becomes a true 3D orbital: clinic as nucleus, four product rings, camera tilting from top-down map to eye-level as sections pass. Products dock into the ring as you meet them.
E · The quiet chess set
Castle and Bishop are chess pieces; nobody says so. Sculpted 3D pieces on a board of practice tiles for the "beyond the clinic" chapter — independence as strategy, never named aloud.
F · Thesis as chapters
The six-step sequence becomes six full-viewport kinetic-type scenes with one evolving diagram — hospital mass dissolving into distributed clinics, then homes. The manifesto finally gets staged.
G · The living map
Replace the marquee with a real-geodata TX/CA map; practices light up in acquisition order, the circle literally widening — "the circle widens to others" as cartography.
H · Boot sequence
The page opens as an OS booting: Foundry mounts, models load, agents come online line by line in mono type — then the GUI (the site) fades in over it. Earns the name "operating system" in five seconds.
I · Vignettes, promoted
Keep the ten vignettes but stage them once each, big, scroll-triggered, with real choreography (the claim physically travels the pipeline; the SOAP note types itself as you pass). Depth of field instead of repetition.
J · Kinetic pivots
Text sections stay still except the italic pivot words — variable-font weight/optical-size animation on shared, clean, slips… Restraint as the signature; the site moves only where the copy does.
Guardrails for all of it: DOM-first content under the WebGL (SEO + accessibility) · prefers-reduced-motion gets a designed static version, not a broken one · mobile gets a designed 2D fallback, not degraded 3D · 60fps budget, lazy-init scenes · light scheme preserved · copy stays verbatim.
