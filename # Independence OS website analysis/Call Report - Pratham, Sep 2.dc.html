02 — THE VALUATION ENGINE

The correction that reshapes the whole first screen.

I proposed asking clinic owners 10–15 questions and returning a price range. Pratham redirected to the Zillow model: predict a range up front from publicly available data, ask nothing first (00:12:10).

The inputs are government sources such as Medicare data, weighted by region, patient panel size, and patient mix. Enter a zip code and the map fills with clinics that already carry an estimated range — whether or not they are for sale. Unlisted clinics carry a notice that the owner has not put the practice on the market (00:12:59).

Questions still exist, but they move to second position. When data is thin, or when the owner disagrees with the automated number because EBITDA or headcount was never in the public record, they add those figures and get a re-evaluation (00:14:08).

MY PROPOSAL — SUPERSEDED
Owner arrives → answers 10–15 questions → receives a range. Nothing to see before the form is filled.
AGREED DIRECTION
Owner arrives → sees a range already computed for their clinic and its neighbours → optionally adds EBITDA, headcount, panel size → range tightens.
03 — BUSINESS SHAPE

Roots Health cannot be the only buyer.

Roots Health is the parent or sister MSO: it acquires clinics, runs their administration and operations, and works out how to raise a given clinic's EBITDA (00:09:58).

A marketplace with one buyer is not a marketplace. Pratham was explicit: OwnCura will bring in other MSOs — including direct competitors of Roots Health — across Texas and California, because the buy side has to be plural before the sell side means anything (00:11:11).

The agent stack follows the same sequence as the website's own argument: prove it inside our clinics first, then sell it through OwnCura to independent clinics we do not own. OwnCura is meant to be the main channel between independent MSOs and the wider US healthcare market (00:16:55).

07 — GTM AND THE ENDGAME

Approved with one correction: this is not a brokerage.

My proposal — weekly feature experiments, 20–30 outbound emails a week to clinic brokers, organic LinkedIn posts on the finances of clinic ownership, no paid media — was approved on the LinkedIn and email tactics (00:24:45). Pratham pushed back on aiming exclusively at traditional brokers: OwnCura is a growth platform, not a listing service, and targeting only brokers narrows it to the one thing it is trying not to be (00:25:46).

The endgame he described is a private-equity structure. Acquire clinics at volume, deploy the verified agent stack across them to lift profit, then show those metrics to institutional investors — he drew the comparison to Blackstone operating with BlackRock as an investor (00:29:58, 00:31:05).

DEFERRED — LATER ITERATIONS

Education and recruitment. An independent physician in an acquisition conversation mentioned spending free time mentoring MBBS and MD students in the clinic; Pratham wants to eventually let students find doctors to train under, and let expanding or retiring physicians find independents to take over a practice (00:21:24). Explicitly not now — the valuation engine and the buyer-side partnerships come first (00:22:39).
